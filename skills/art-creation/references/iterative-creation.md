Help a non-expert discover and express intent through visible comparisons and feedback. Start with rough words, sketches, references, or clips. [Practice review](practice-review.md) explains the background.

## 1. Establish a useful starting point

Extract purpose, medium, essential content, constraints, stated likes/dislikes, and unresolved preferences. Begin with a reasonable assumption or a question that determines the next useful experiment.

Use [style analysis](style-analysis.md) for a full reference report or decision-relevant findings during exploration. For ambiguous language, [Intent and evidence](intent-and-evidence.md) helps turn possible interpretations into observable comparisons.

## 2. Analyze and construct with language and programs first

Inspect available source, text, geometry, pixels, or timing and choose an inspectable representation. Start at the fidelity needed to settle the question and increase detail as the direction develops.

Use multimodal or world-model assistance for perceptual questions that remain after programmatic inspection.

Use [Tool capabilities](tool-capabilities.md) for specialist rendering or setup, and consider [optional delegation](delegation.md) for independent work.

## 3. Compare meaningful directions

For an open idea, normally show three clearly different directions, adapting the count to the request. Vary meaningful axes such as structure, rhythm, silhouette, material, or motion while retaining the brief's content and accepted identity.

Give candidates stable IDs and brief explanations of their mechanisms. Display them under comparable conditions, using playback for temporal questions. Record changes, seeds, assets, scale, and timing as relevant.

## 4. Get a reaction

After showing the candidates, ask one compact question that resolves the current uncertainty. Use the response to choose the next direction: a selection, rejection, everyday description, or combination of parts. Explain useful vocabulary through the visible difference.

## 5. Refine and preserve choices

Record what to keep, reject, or investigate. Compare one or two unresolved dimensions, keep the accepted baseline visible, and try another mechanism when needed. Expose a few controls that help the decision.

A compact decision record can contain version, preview, intended changes, retained and rejected choices, and the next question. Save sources and recipes for replay and continued refinement. If every direction misses, revisit the interpretation.

## 6. Converge and hand off

Deliver the requested level: a direction, prompt, editable prototype, or finished asset. Compare the output with accepted choices and task requirements.

Provide the visible result, a medium-appropriate implementation prompt, observable acceptance criteria, and editable source or recipe. Identify inherited reference features, use-specific changes, and new suggestions.

## Reusable starting prompt

Execute this instruction in Chinese unless the user chooses another language:

    Help me discover and express a creative result from my rough words, sketch, reference, or clip. Begin with a useful interpretation of my purpose, medium, and preferences.
    Use inspectable language and programs first, with specialist perceptual assistance for unresolved visual questions.
    Show labeled, comparable directions that test meaningful differences, explain them in ordinary language, and ask one small feedback question.
    Preserve my accepted choices while refining unresolved ones. Hand off the visible result, creative brief, implementation prompt, and editable sources.
