# Intent and Evidence

Use this when an aesthetic phrase admits several interpretations or an iteration needs a clearer test.

Connect the user's phrase to an object and context, possible interpretations, observable properties or relationships, a construction choice, and a preview that distinguishes them. An atom is the smallest meaningful decision at the current stage. Include dependencies between atoms and accepted choices.

| Record when useful | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Object, region, state, and parent intent | Keep the interpretation tied to what the user means |
| Observation, hypothesis, preference, or unresolved choice | Relate the interpretation to its source |
| Baseline, proposed change, control, and invariant | Make the experiment interpretable |
| Preview and comparison conditions | Make the result inspectable |

Measurements may be numeric, categorical, relational, temporal, or anchored human comparisons. Include units and the measured region or state where relevant. Label measured, estimated, and proposed values; inferred intent remains a hypothesis. Use user feedback to refine aesthetic targets.

Choose previews that reveal the property being discussed: composition, material, attachment, or motion. If feedback keeps rejecting the same property, reconsider the interpretation or mechanism.

Tie each check to the intended object, relationship, and state. Compare with the baseline to inspect the intended change and retained choices.

For continuity, keep a compact decision record linked to the current artifacts and recipe.
